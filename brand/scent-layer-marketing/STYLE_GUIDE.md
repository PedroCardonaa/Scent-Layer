# Scent Layer — Style Guide

The rules that keep Scent Layer's copy recognizable across product pages, captions, emails, and blog posts.

## Capitalization

- **Headlines:** Sentence case. "How to find your signature scent" — not "How To Find Your Signature Scent."
- **Product titles on site:** Sentence case for the descriptor, Title Case for the fragrance name itself. Example: "5ml decant — *Baccarat Rouge 540*."
- **House and perfume names:** Always preserve the brand's preferred capitalization (e.g., "Maison Francis Kurkdjian," "Le Labo," "Diptyque," "By Kilian").
- **Concentrations:** EDP, EDT, EDC, parfum — uppercase, no periods. Spell out on first use in long content: "eau de parfum (EDP)."

## Punctuation

- **Oxford comma:** Always.
- **Em dashes:** Use freely — they fit the voice. No spaces around them.
- **Exclamation marks:** One max per piece. Use only when truly warranted.
- **Quotation marks:** American style (commas and periods inside).
- **Ellipses:** Avoid in marketing copy.

## Contractions and Person

- **Contractions:** Use them. "We're," "you'll," "it's." Sounds human.
- **Pronouns:** First person plural ("we") for the brand. Second person ("you") for the reader. Avoid third-person institutional voice ("Scent Layer believes...").
- **Inclusive pronouns:** Default to "they" when referring to a generic customer or wearer.

## Numbers and Units

- **In prose:** Spell out one through nine. Numerals for 10 and up.
- **Sizes and quantities:** Always numerals — "2ml sample," "5ml decant," "10ml travel."
- **Currency:** "$14" not "$14.00" unless cents are non-zero. No "USD" unless in international context.
- **Percent:** Numeral plus "%" — "50%."

## Fragrance Terminology

- **First use of a fragrance:** Include the house. "Tom Ford's *Tobacco Vanille*," not just "*Tobacco Vanille*" on first mention.
- **Italicize fragrance names** on first mention in a piece; plain text after.
- **Notes lists:** Top → heart → base, separated by em dashes or arrows. "Bergamot and cardamom → rose and saffron → amberwood and musk."
- **Avoid the words:** "elixir," "potion," "enchanting," "mysterious," "alluring," "intoxicating," "olfactory journey," "symphony of notes," "bouquet of essences," "captivating." These are perfume-marketing clichés.
- **Acceptable sensory words:** Warm, smoky, sharp, green, powdery, creamy, dry, sweet, peppery, ozonic, animalic, woody, balsamic, gourmand, citrus-forward, soliflore. These are real descriptors with real meaning.

## Tone by Channel

| Channel | Tone notes |
|---|---|
| Product page | Confident, sensorial-but-specific, ends with a clear "who it's for." |
| Blog / journal | Educational, conversational, lightly opinionated. ~700–1,200 words. |
| Instagram caption | Punchy, hook first sentence. 1–3 short paragraphs. |
| TikTok script / on-screen text | Even punchier. First three seconds must hook. |
| Email subject line | Curiosity or specificity. Never ALL CAPS, never "Don't miss out!" |
| Email body | Conversational, scannable. One CTA per email. |
| Customer service | Warm, accountable, never defensive. |

## Formatting

- **Lists:** Use sparingly in long-form. Bullet points kill rhythm in product copy.
- **Bold:** For genuine emphasis only. Never to highlight half the sentence.
- **Italics:** Fragrance names, foreign terms on first use, occasional emphasis.
- **Links:** Descriptive anchor text. Never "click here."

## What "Good Scent Layer Copy" Looks Like

**Good:**
> "Tom Ford's *Tobacco Vanille* is the bottle people lie about being too rich for. It's spiced tobacco and burnt sugar — the smell of a wood-paneled bar in December. Not subtle. Not for everyone. But the people who love it really love it. 5ml, $22."

**Bad:**
> "Indulge in the enchanting elixir of Tom Ford Tobacco Vanille — an intoxicating olfactory journey through a captivating bouquet of mysterious essences that will leave you and those around you spellbound!"

## Emoji Policy

- **Social:** Sparingly. One per caption max, only when it adds meaning. No 🌿✨💫 perfume-influencer pile-ups.
- **Email subject lines:** Avoid.
- **Blog / product pages:** Never.

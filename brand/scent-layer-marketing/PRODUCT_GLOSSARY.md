# Scent Layer — Product Glossary

A reference for terms that show up constantly in Scent Layer copy. Use these definitions when explaining concepts in long-form content, and keep the spellings consistent.

## Format / Size Terms

- **Decant** — A portion of fragrance transferred from an original bottle into a smaller atomizer for sampling or travel.
- **Sample** — Smallest size, typically 2ml. Enough for several wears.
- **Travel** — Mid-size, typically 10ml. Enough for a few weeks of daily wear.
- **Atomizer** — The spray bottle used for decants.
- **Splash bottle** — A bottle without a spray mechanism. Rare in modern decanting.

## Concentration Terms

- **Parfum (extrait de parfum)** — Highest oil concentration (typically 20–40%). Longest-lasting, often deepest projection.
- **Eau de parfum (EDP)** — High oil concentration (15–20%). Industry standard for modern niche fragrance.
- **Eau de toilette (EDT)** — Medium concentration (5–15%). Lighter, often more citrus-forward, shorter wear.
- **Eau de cologne (EDC)** — Lightest concentration (2–5%). Mostly citrus and herbal compositions.

## Performance Terms

- **Projection** — How far the scent radiates off the wearer ("how loud" it is).
- **Sillage** — The trail of scent left behind as the wearer moves through space. (Pronounced "see-yazh.")
- **Longevity** — How long the fragrance lasts on skin.
- **Skin scent** — The phase where a fragrance sits close to the skin rather than projecting.
- **Dry-down** — The final phase of a fragrance, dominated by base notes.

## Composition Terms

- **Top notes** — The first impression. Citrus, aromatic, sparkling notes that fade within 15–30 minutes.
- **Heart notes** — The body of the fragrance. Florals, spices, and rich aromatics that emerge after the top notes settle.
- **Base notes** — The foundation. Woods, resins, musks, ambers that linger longest.
- **Accord** — A combination of notes that creates a single recognizable impression (e.g., "ambery accord," "leather accord").
- **Soliflore** — A fragrance built around a single flower note.
- **Note pyramid** — The conventional way of describing a fragrance: top → heart → base.

## Family / Style Terms

- **Gourmand** — Edible, dessert-like fragrances. Vanilla, caramel, chocolate, coffee.
- **Amber** — Warm, slightly sweet, resinous. (Formerly "oriental," now considered outdated.)
- **Chypre** — Mossy, woody, citrus-and-floral compositions. (Pronounced "sheep-ruh.")
- **Fougère** — Aromatic, herbal, slightly soapy. Often described as "classic masculine," though gender-neutral. (Pronounced "foo-zhair.")
- **Aquatic / ozonic** — Marine, watery, fresh-air style.
- **Animalic** — Musks, ouds, leathers, civet — scents that smell warm-bodied or sensual.
- **Aldehydic** — Sparkling, soapy-clean, slightly metallic. (Think *Chanel No. 5*.)

## Industry Terms

- **House** — A perfume brand. ("Niche houses" = smaller, often founder-led brands.)
- **Designer fragrance** — Fragrance from a fashion house or mainstream luxury brand (Dior, YSL, Tom Ford, Versace).
- **Niche fragrance** — Fragrance from independent or smaller houses focused specifically on perfumery (Maison Francis Kurkdjian, Parfums de Marly, Initio, Xerjoff).
- **Indie** — Smaller still — often single-perfumer brands operating outside traditional distribution.
- **Nose / perfumer** — The person who composes the fragrance. (Both terms are used interchangeably.)
- **Reformulation** — When a fragrance is reissued with different ingredients, often due to IFRA regulations or cost. A frequent topic among collectors.

## Avoid

- "Perfume" as the only word — use "fragrance" or "scent" more often for variety.
- "Toilet water" — never. Use eau de toilette.
- "Oriental" as a family descriptor — say "amber" instead.
- "Ladies" or "gents" — say "feminine-leaning," "masculine-leaning," or just describe the scent.

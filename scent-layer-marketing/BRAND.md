## Company Snapshot

- **Brand:** Scent Layer
- **What we sell:** Decants and full bottles of niche and designer fragrances. Customers can sample widely through affordable decants, then graduate to full bottles of the scents they love.
- **Core promise:** Make discovering, learning about, and wearing fragrance feel approachable, confident, and personal — without the gatekeeping of traditional perfumery.
- **Why we exist:** Full bottles are expensive and intimidating. Sephora samples are tiny and limited to mainstream picks. Scent Layer gives anyone — from a first-time fragrance buyer to a seasoned collector — a low-risk way to try the perfumes the internet is actually talking about, and the confidence to know what suits them.
- **What "Scent Layer" means:** Fragrance is layered — top, heart, base. So is taste. We help customers build a personal scent wardrobe one layer at a time.

## One-line Positioning

Scent Layer is the friendlier, more curious way into fragrance — affordable decants of niche and designer perfumes, paired with the kind of plain-English education that turns a curious shopper into a confident wearer.

## Audience

Scent Layer is for anyone who's curious about fragrance, regardless of expertise level. We design our voice and content so that a first-time buyer and a seasoned collector both feel welcome reading the same caption.

Three primary personas to keep in mind when drafting:

1. **The Curious Beginner** — knows they want to "smell good" or find a signature scent but is overwhelmed by terminology (EDP vs. EDT, sillage, projection). Needs reassurance, plain language, and a clear next step.
2. **The Fragrance Explorer** — has 3–10 bottles already, watches FragranceTok / Reddit r/fragrance, wants to try buzzy niche houses without spending $300 blind. Needs honest notes, accurate descriptions, and recommendations beyond the obvious.
3. **The Collector** — knows their Ouds from their Ambers, follows specific noses and houses, decants to extend their collection. Needs accuracy, respect, and access to harder-to-find picks.

See `AUDIENCE.md` for fuller persona detail.

## Brand Voice

If Scent Layer were a person, they'd be the friend at the perfume counter who actually knows what they're talking about — warm, opinionated, never snobby, and genuinely excited to help you find something you love. They translate the jargon, share strong opinions when asked, and never make you feel dumb for not knowing what "chypre" means.

### Voice Attributes

**Confident — not arrogant**
- We are: clear, certain, willing to give a real recommendation, willing to say "this one is divisive"
- We are not: hedging, wishy-washy, or pretending every fragrance is for everyone
- Sounds like: "If you love vanilla but want grown-up vanilla, this is the one."
- Does NOT sound like: "This fragrance might be something some people could potentially enjoy."

**Educational — not condescending**
- We are: explaining terms in passing, demystifying the snobbery, treating the reader as smart
- We are not: dumbing things down, lecturing, or assuming the reader is a novice
- Sounds like: "EDP just means it lasts longer than EDT — more oil, more staying power."
- Does NOT sound like: "Now class, let's learn about concentration levels..."

**Warm and conversational**
- We are: chatting like a friend, using contractions, asking the occasional question
- We are not: stiff, corporate, hashtag-stuffed, or trying too hard to be quirky
- Sounds like: "Tried this on a rainy Tuesday and didn't take it off all week."
- Does NOT sound like: "Indulge in the olfactive symphony of our latest acquisition."

**Sensorial — but specific**
- We are: vivid in describing how a scent actually wears, what it reminds us of, who it's for
- We are not: relying on tired perfume clichés ("musky enchantment," "mysterious allure")
- Sounds like: "Warm vanilla and smoke. Reads expensive on a cashmere sweater."
- Does NOT sound like: "An enchanting bouquet of mysterious essences."

### Voice Spectrum

| Spectrum | Where Scent Layer sits |
|---|---|
| Formality | Casual, conversational |
| Authority | Knowledgeable peer (not professor, not influencer) |
| Emotion | Warm |
| Complexity | Plain-language with terms explained inline |

## Messaging Pillars

See `MESSAGING.md` for full treatments. The four pillars:

1. **Try before you commit.** Full bottles are an investment. Decants are how you find what's actually worth it.
2. **Niche shouldn't mean inaccessible.** We carry the houses fragrance lovers are talking about — without the gatekeeping or the markup.
3. **Build your scent wardrobe.** One signature isn't the goal anymore. A scent for the office, the date, the gym, the trip — that's the goal.
4. **Learn as you wear.** Every order is a chance to learn one new thing about fragrance: a note, a house, a perfumer, a term.

## What We Don't Sound Like

- We don't use "elixir," "potion," "enchanting," "mysterious," "alluring," or "intoxicating" without irony.
- We don't gatekeep. No "if you know, you know" energy. No condescending the mall-fragrance lover.
- We don't fake authority. If a scent is divisive or polarizing, we say so.
- We don't oversell. "It smells incredible" is fine. "Compliments guaranteed" is not.
- We don't fake scarcity. No "almost gone!!" unless it's true.

## Quick Style Rules

Full rules in `STYLE_GUIDE.md`. The essentials:

- **Tone:** Sentence case for headlines and product titles. Title Case only for proper names of fragrances, houses, and perfumers.
- **Punctuation:** Oxford comma. Em dashes for asides — like this. One exclamation mark max, used sparingly.
- **Contractions:** Use them. "It's," "we've," "you'll."
- **Numbers:** Spell out one through nine in prose. Numerals for sizes ("5ml decant," "2ml sample").
- **Fragrance terms:** Italicize French/foreign names on first use (*Baccarat Rouge 540*), then plain text. Always include the house when first naming a fragrance (e.g., "Maison Francis Kurkdjian's *Baccarat Rouge 540*").
- **Concentration shorthand:** EDP, EDT, EDC, parfum — uppercase, no periods. Spell out on first use in long-form content ("eau de parfum (EDP)").
- **Notes lists:** Top → heart → base. Use em dashes or arrows, not "/". E.g., "saffron and rose → amberwood → musk."
- **Emoji policy:** Sparingly on social. None in long-form content or email subject lines.

## Channels We Care About

Right now Scent Layer focuses on two channels — see `CHANNELS.md` for full guidance:

- **Social media** — primarily Instagram and TikTok. Short-form video reviews, "what's in your decant box" content, fragrance education explainers, and trend reactions.

Email is a planned secondary channel — keep it on the roadmap when planning, but don't lead with it.

## Compliance and Legal

- **No medical claims.** Fragrance affects mood; it does not "heal" anything.
- **Allergen disclosure** required on product pages (IFRA / EU compliance for international shipping).
- **Honest sourcing language.** Decants are repackaged from authentic full bottles. Always describe accurately; never claim "official partnership" with a house unless one exists.
- **No counterfeits, ever.** Authenticity is non-negotiable and should be reinforced in copy where relevant.
- **Influencer / affiliate disclosure** required on social.
